About the Study

This study examines how people interact with information presented through digital interfaces while completing a task. You were invited because you meet the study's eligibility requirements; no specialized knowledge or prior experience is required. Participation is voluntary, and you may stop at any time without penalty.

What to Expect

  • You will play multiple games of Tic-Tac-Toe in a web browser on a mobile device.
  • Different elements and information may appear within the interface while you play.
  • Interact with the game and anything that appears on the screen as you normally would. There are no right or wrong ways to interact.
  • Your interactions with the interface will be recorded for research purposes.
  • After the games, you will complete a short post-study survey.
  • Participation will take approximately 20–25 minutes.

Potential Risks & Benefits

This study has minimal risks. You may experience minor frustration or inconvenience while completing the task. You can stop participating at any time without penalty. You should not expect any direct benefit, but your participation may help us better understand how people interact with digital interfaces.

Confidentiality

We will not ask for your name or other directly identifying information. Your responses will be associated with a randomly generated participant ID and stored as coded research data. Access will be limited to the research team and authorized individuals. Results may be reported in aggregate, without identifying individual participants. While we will take reasonable steps to protect your information, confidentiality cannot be guaranteed.

Please save or write down your participant ID for your records.

Study Complete

Thank you for participating!

This study investigated what makes smartphone notifications noticeable while people are engaged in another task.

During the study, notifications varied in three ways:

  • Visual prominence - how visually noticeable the notification was.
  • Animation — how the notification appeared on screen.
  • Duration — how long the notification remained visible.

We recorded how you interacted with each notification, including whether you dismissed or opened it and how quickly you responded.

The purpose of collecting these responses is to better understand how notification design influences noticeability during everyday activities.
